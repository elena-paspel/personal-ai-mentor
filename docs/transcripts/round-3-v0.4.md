[Brief clarification] A common structure, whether this is a standalone review article or the background section of a research paper:
1. Introduction: topic, why it matters for nursing, and the scope and aim.
2. For a standalone review, methods: databases, search terms, and inclusion and exclusion criteria.
3. Body: organised by theme, not study by study, comparing findings.
4. Gaps and limitations.
5. Conclusion: implications for practice, or the question your study answers.

[Brief clarification]

- Scoping review: maps what evidence exists on a broader topic and where the gaps are, usually without appraisal.

Both need a documented, reproducible search and normally take weeks to months, not days. Please verify this: Munn et al. (2018, BMC Medical Research Methodology) is a guide to exactly this choice, and your journal lists the review types it accepts.
